\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:ev-threats}

\emph{Construct validity.} Effects, types and joint feasibility are scored against labels produced by LLMs, not by
human annotators; execution confirms these labels only on the state changes of 30 tools of six reference
servers, which are maintained by the protocol's authors and are not adversarial. Reachability of injected actions
is decided offline from labels; no injected instruction was executed, and whether an agent follows it, or whether
runtime enforcement blocks it, was not measured. Group recall rewards gold tools rather than functional
sufficiency, and the strict evaluator, although more accurate than the coarse one on hand-built cases (7 of 10
against 2), lacks scope, credential and declassification information. No toolkit was executed end to end, so task
success, argument validity, recovery after execution failures and end-to-end latency are outside the evidence
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ev-evidence}). QoS effects are shown on generated instances only.

\emph{Internal validity.} One model family provides the two labellings, the typing, the analyst and the profiles,
so their errors may correlate; the label swap bounds the effect of their disagreement but not of shared errors.
The verifier is simulated with controlled error rates. Each LLM role was sampled once, and one Opus calibration
batch (45 items) is missing. The baselines are adaptations sharing a TF-IDF and SVD encoder (a stronger encoder
could not be installed): COLT without encoder fine-tuning, ToolScope without its cross-encoder, MCP-Zero with one
round of requests. All retrieval methods were tuned on the same 60 development requests with grids of 1--16
configurations; the variant selected for \pamcp{} on these requests did not transfer to the test set, a reminder
that 60 requests are a small selection sample.

\emph{External validity.} The registry has 569 tools from two benchmarks, with sparse annotations and no QoS data;
larger registries are cloned or generated, and latencies are measured on two virtual CPUs. The provider did not
expose exact model version strings; the measured profiles and the reference servers' definitions, which all
changed since the benchmark snapshot, may not hold for later versions. \emph{Statistical conclusion validity.}
Confirmatory comparisons are few and Holm-corrected within families fixed in advance; every other comparison is
exploratory, and resampling one benchmark sample measures stability, not replication.

\emph{What remains open.} Three experiments would close the main gaps: human annotation of a stratified sample of
tools by two annotators, executed multi-step compositions over isolated MCP servers including adversarial ones,
and faithful reproductions of the strongest baselines with their original encoders. The released artifact
contains the annotation sheet, the probing harness used for the reference servers and the scripts of every
experiment above, so that these can be run against the same data and scoring.
