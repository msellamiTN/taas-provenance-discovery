## 3. Changes elsewhere in the manuscript

The manuscript refers to "the evaluation plan" in five places and announces the evaluation in the Conclusion.
Suggested replacements (labels from §1):

1. Section 6 (two LLM roles): "the evaluation plan compares two roles on one model, two roles on different model
   families, one integrated role, and the analyst role alone without repair proposals." →
   "comparing two roles on one model, two roles on different model families, one integrated role and the analyst
   role alone without repair proposals is left to future work; Section~\ref{sec:ev} uses the analyst role of one
   model and deterministic repairs."
2. Section 6.1 (feasibility): "probing applies to runnable, credential-free servers, which the evaluation will
   count," → "probing applies to runnable, credential-free servers (Section~\ref{sec:ev} simulates its outcome,
   because no server was executed),"
3. Section 6.3 (cost weights): "sensitivity to the weights and to umin is part of the evaluation plan." →
   "sensitivity to the weights and to $u_{\min}$ is reported in Section~\ref{sec:ev-rq4}."
4. Section 6.3 (sanitised manifest): "its effect on selection accuracy is part of the evaluation plan." →
   "Section~\ref{sec:ev-ablation} measures its effect on poisoned descriptions; its effect on selection accuracy
   was not measured."
5. Section 7 (imperfect verification): "which the evaluation plan measures." → "which
   Section~\ref{sec:ev-rq2} quantifies."
6. Eq. (6): the experiments add a relevance term $w_{\mathrm{rel}}(1-\hat{s}(e))$ to the cost (selected on the
   development split; without it, the cheapest type-compatible tool wins). The setup paragraph states this; you
   may instead add the term to Eq. (6) in Section 6.3 and drop the sentence.
7. Conclusion, last paragraph ("The evaluation, to be reported separately, will test four hypotheses …"), replace
   with:

   > The evaluation of Section 8 supports part of this picture and qualifies the rest. On the 569 tools of the
   > public registries, a fifth of the tools are not read-only and 44 carry annotations, so reading missing
   > annotations as safe exposes 42.5\% of the requests to an unsafe tool; verified evidence removes that
   > exposure at a utility cost of 3.6 points, and remains preferable to lexical cues only while the verifier's
   > false-accept rate stays below 10\% (5\% when its errors are random). Certified toolkits hold 1.2 to 1.5
   > tools and leave no out-of-scope action reachable in the offline analysis, mostly through eligibility, but
   > they do not raise joint feasibility over eligible baselines and they cover fewer gold tools than retrievers
   > that expose more tools. Typed retrieval from goal contracts degrades as the registry grows when goal and
   > tool typings disagree, the main limitation found. Task success under execution, sandbox-observed effects,
   > the LLM role configurations and experience reuse remain to be measured on runnable servers.

## 5. Deviations from the protocol (stated in the section)

* B1 has 81 tools (the MCPToolBench++ tools that are not in LiveMCPTool, plus every gold tool), not 87.
* Dense encoder: TF-IDF + truncated SVD (128 dimensions) instead of BGE-M3, for every method.
* Baselines are adaptations: MCP-Zero with one round of recorded requests; COLT's collaborative stage as
  parameter-free graph propagation, no encoder fine-tuning; ToolScope without its cross-encoder reranker;
  CMTF with relevance tie-breaking, top-40 tools per goal, depth ≤ 6 and ≤ 5,000 expansions. None was tuned.
* Labels: two LLM labellings (Claude Sonnet for typing and simulated evidence, Claude Opus blind for scoring),
  not human labels; $J_B$'s unsafe set is contained in $J_A$'s, so verified exposure is zero by construction.
* E12 uses a 64-item calibration set and three models, not the protocol's full corpus.
* E15 uses cloned contracts and generated cover instances; the exact search uses the engine's default budget of
  20,000 nodes.
* E5 at δ = 0 was run with one seed (five for δ > 0).
